# Shipley-aligned Review Capability Library

This is a THINK Managed Teams implementation of the review points described in Shipley's published Winning Color Team Reviews material, not an assertion that Shipley endorses or certifies this product. Use official concepts and objective definitions. Tailor the effort, number of participants, and artifacts to the pursuit.

Sources:
- https://www.shipleywins.com/corporate-training/winning-color-team-reviews
- https://www.shipleywins.com/blogs/color-team-reviews

## Architecture and ownership

Reviews are reusable capabilities embedded into the existing Research, Assessment, Strategy, Draft, QA/Review and Output workflow, not seven additional mandatory pipeline stages and not seven new Digital Employees. Maya orchestrates. Chase provides source-grounded research, Priya reviews finance, Porter leads evaluator/strategy challenges, Quinn develops content plans and approved revisions, Diego independently verifies compliance and final readiness, and Blair supports funder-specific work as appropriate.

Review points can overlap and execute in parallel. A review is not automatically an approval: only the authorized human makes pursue, price, final-content and submittal decisions. No reviewer approves work merely because their own earlier output passed. A human Strategist can allocate real independent reviewers for material or conflicted work.

## Review selection and depth

Apply every *relevant* review objective, but do not schedule every color team as a separate meeting.

- **Light**: short, low-risk responses; focused source checklist, inline issue comments, one pass, compact sign-off.
- **Standard**: ordinary competitive proposals; documented requirements/evaluation mapping, strategy and priced response review, issue register, independent QA.
- **Comprehensive**: high-risk/high-value or complex requirements; independent reviewers, detailed source-to-claim traceability, multiple review passes and explicit approval records.
- Escalate for rejection-sensitive requirements, sensitive pricing, missing required letters, unusual formats, external linked instructions, complex teaming, or material contradictions regardless of page count. Escalation can target one review without expanding all reviews.

Before starting, record: organization being represented, solicitation and authoritative versions, submission deadline/time zone, available source links and their verification status, stage(s) requested, owner/reviewer, client approver, selected depth and reasons.

## Color team capabilities and timing

| Review | Trigger and objective | Lead/support | Minimum output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blue Team #1 | Early capture: challenge initial capture strategy, customer issues, solution, discriminator assumptions | Porter; Chase | Strategy gaps and questions for the human capture owner |
| Black Hat | Competitive uncertainty: predict competitors' likely offers from sourced evidence; never fabricate opponents | Porter; Chase | Competing hypotheses, confidence, countermeasures |
| Blue Team #2 | Capture update: verify refined plan and proposed solution before full response execution | Porter; Chase, Priya | Reconciled strategy and approval questions |
| Pink Team | Before writing: review storyboards, annotated outline, mockups/content plans for solicitation coverage, customer focus and win strategy | Quinn; Porter, Diego | Outline/content-plan findings and drafting authorizations |
| Green Team | When pricing exists/changes: verify cost/price solution, accuracy, affordability, margin/risk and submission presentation | Priya; Diego | Pricing/financial issues, assumptions, human pricing approval request |
| Red Team | Once evaluable draft exists: simulate published evaluation criteria, customer concerns and competitive position; examine price where relevant | Porter; Diego | Evidence-based evaluator feedback, likely scoring weaknesses, ranked fixes |
| Gold Team | After Red/Green actions: confirm necessary corrections and approved price, and request the authorized proposal submittal decision | Diego; Maya, Priya | PASS / CONDITIONAL / HOLD with open exceptions and approver |
| White Team | Post-submission/award: capture lessons learned and feedback through contract award | Maya; all contributors | Lessons, reusable assets, process improvements |

Run a second Red Review against the *latest* controlled document revision when substantive edits arrive; compare every prior finding to the new text rather than reissuing closed findings. Green may be run in parallel with draft reviews; substantive budget changes reopen Green and can reopen Red/Gold.

For an already-drafted or late-arriving response, use retrospective requirements and compliance triage followed by Red, Green and Gold as appropriate. **Do not call a full-draft compliance scrub an ordinary Pink Team**; record it as a retrospective/compressed review and distinguish from pre-draft Pink.

## Universal finding contract

Keep one canonical register per pursuit, with:
- ID, review color, severity (CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), status (OPEN/IN PROGRESS/RESOLVED/WAIVED/UNVERIFIED), evidence confidence
- exact source title/version/URL or attached file section, controlling requirement vs advisory material, exact proposal location/text
- why it matters to compliance, evaluation or economics; recommended action; owner; reviewer; approver; due time
- decision/evidence of correction and document revision/time last verified

**Confirmed noncompliance** requires an identified governing provision and observed violation. **UNVERIFIED** means unavailable, conflicting or unconfirmed source. **Competitive quality** is advisory, not automatically a compliance failure. Reconcile contradictions among summary, RFP, incorporated instructions, addenda, appendices, forms, hyperlinks and portal requirements. Never claim links were checked if inaccessible; cite any source not retrieved as unverified. Check document templates separately from executable certificates or letters; visible placeholders in a sample are not inherently submission defects, but missing mandatory attachments are.

Do not invent commitments, people, costs, dates, citations, external endorsements or evaluator scores. Do not silently overwrite the customer's text. When collaborating live, prefer precise anchored comments or clearly marked proposed language, re-read before edits, guard revisions against concurrent changes, and preserve customer approval.

## Review handoff and submission QA

1. Maya assigns reviewer(s) and a risk-adjusted scope.
2. Review lead produces findings and links each to authoritative sources and latest proposal version.
3. Human/content owner disposes each finding; reviewer independently validates corrections.
4. Red/Green material reopeners return to the appropriate review; Gold cannot pass while critical requirements remain open or unverified without an explicit human risk decision.
5. Gold requests *human* approval of final proposal and final price.
6. After Gold, Diego checks the exact approved export, tables, pagination, attachments, signatures, upload fields and actual submission instructions. This **submission QA** is a THINK operational check, not another Shipley color team.
7. White records lessons after submission and, where available, evaluation feedback/award outcome.

## Separation of duties

Quinn should not be sole reviewer of Quinn's draft. Porter should not exclusively grade a strategy Porter authored. Priya's pricing analysis should get a second QA check. Diego can flag and verify but not unilaterally approve client pricing or submission. In Light engagements, a human may combine review sessions but must still record conflicts, unresolved risks and human approval.
